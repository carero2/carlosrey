Research

My work sits between behavioral science and data science. I use behavioral experiments and computational models to understand how people respond to losses and uncertainty, and language models and machine learning both as tools to measure human behavior and as systems people interact with.

  • Humans and LLMs

    How people learn from, experience and come to trust large language models.

  • Losses and decision making

    Cognitive processes behind losses, from reflection and attention to value perception and consumer choice.

  • Behavioral theory meets ML

    NLP, LLMs and social media analysis to model psychological processes and improve predictions of human choice.

Publications

Working papers

  • Mapping the gap between laboratory and real-world losses

    with Dirk Wulff and Tomás Lejarraga

    How people perceive real-world losses, and whether laboratory findings on loss aversion generalize beyond monetary tasks, combining experiments with LLM-based literature mapping.

    Working paperLLMsLoss aversion

  • Do losses promote more reflection than equivalent gains?

    with Antoni Rubí-Barceló and Tomás Lejarraga

    Two behavioral experiments and linear mixed models show that losses increase reflection time but not reflection quality.

    Working paperExperimentsMixed models

  • The role of losses in rapid value-based choice

    with Sebastian Horn, Thorsten Pachur and Tomás Lejarraga

    Drift-diffusion modelling to test whether losses lower drift rates or widen decision boundaries.

    Working paperDrift-diffusion

  • Loss aversion: distribution, lifespan trajectory and predictive validity

    with Ralph Hertwig and Tomás Lejarraga

    German Socio-Economic Panel data to estimate the prevalence of loss aversion and its links with demographics, household income and investment decisions.

    Working paperPanel data
